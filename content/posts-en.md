# English social copy

## Post 1 — Carousel opener

**It is not only how much electricity we use. Timing can matter too.**

Dishwashers, washing machines and EV charging may offer flexibility—but not every household or appliance has the same options. Start with one device, check the information available from your tariff, app or device, and test a time that fits your routine.

**One flexible appliance. One better hour.**

Note: price effects depend on the tariff. Safety and convenience come first.

## Post 2 — Myth versus fact

**Myth:** Electricity is always cheaper and greener at night.  
**Fact:** There is no universal best hour.

Tariffs, local system conditions and generation mix vary. A sound choice starts with information relevant to the household—not a generic clock time.

## Post 3 — Checklist

Four questions before shifting a load:

1. Is this appliance genuinely flexible?
2. Can it run safely on a schedule?
3. Does the tariff, app or device show a useful window?
4. Does the change fit the household routine?

If one answer is “no”, not shifting is also a valid decision.

## Post 4 — Corporate context

The energy transition does not happen only at power plants. Networks, digital information, flexible demand and dependable infrastructure have to work together.

Households are part of that system—not the sole solution to it.

## Post 5 — One-week reflection

One week. One appliance. Three questions:

- Was the new routine convenient?
- Were the available tariff or consumption signals clear?
- Would you keep doing it?

An energy solution has to work in daily life, not only on a diagram.

## Post 6 — Transparency

What we can say: flexible demand can be one part of a more electrified energy system.

What we cannot promise: that every shifted load automatically reduces a bill or emissions.

Transparent communication is not fine print. It is part of the product.
